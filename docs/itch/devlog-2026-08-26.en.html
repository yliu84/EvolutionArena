Until this update the game had exactly one verb: close the distance, hit the thing. Whatever body you evolved into, that was it.

Now each evolution line has its own skill on Q, and each one is a different answer to the same problem: there is distance between you and the thing that wants you dead.

  • Pounce (Fang) — delete the gap. 9 m, 26 damage, 7 s cooldown. You arrive in a thin hide; that is the trade.
  • Bulwark (Shell) — do not close faster. Close anyway. 3.2 s at 65% damage reduction, and it shoves the ring of things crowding you.
  • Spore Bloom (Swarm) — never close at all. Spit an orb from 11 m; it strikes a body and leaves a poison that eats six instalments of health while you walk away.

Spore Bloom got rebuilt

The first version dropped a patch of burning ground. Arithmetically fine, and completely invisible: damage came off at a fraction of a point per frame so no number ever appeared, the effect was on the floor so nothing about the creature changed, and the animal did not move when you cast it.

The feedback was one sentence:

"Is this even a skill?"

Now it is an orb that leaves the mouth, crosses the gap and lands on a body. That body turns green and coughs up a -10 every 0.6 seconds, six times, while you are busy with something else. Anything standing close enough gets caught in the cloud.

Monsters finally have attacks you can see

Every ordinary creature used to announce its blow the same way: a red circle on the ground that filled up and flashed. That was the entire attack presentation — a lunging Fang hunter, a plated Shell ram and a Swarm cloud were visually the same event.

  • Fang throws claw streaks along its facing, and no ring at all. The danger is a direction, and a circle can never say which way a lunge came from.
  • Shell puts down a shock at its full reach, with dust lifted off the rim — the edge is the part you have to read.
  • Swarm raises a low spore cloud: dimmest and longest of the three, because a swarm blow is a condition you are standing in.

One rule underneath all of it: the area drawn is the area tested. The effect is handed the same reach number the damage check uses. Drawn wider, it teaches you a lie; drawn narrower, the creature feels like it cheats.

Also fixed

  • Pouncing at something behind you played the whole leap backwards. It turns during the crouch now, so every airborne frame is head-first.
  • The plated Shell body was animated as a planted slam but moved by a gecko's leap arc — half a metre off the ground, tilted eight degrees. That is the "body deforms and gets hauled upward" people kept reporting.
  • Skills refused to fire at bosses. Two of three lines had no verb in the fight that decides a run.

Next: ranged enemies. The three skills were designed against them from the start — close, endure, deny.
